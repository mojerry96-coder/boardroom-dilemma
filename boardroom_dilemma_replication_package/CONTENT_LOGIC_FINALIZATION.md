# 3. Exact evidence-document content

## Evidence 01 — Incident Report

### Purpose in the simulation
Confirms the fatal incident, the bypassed interlock, and that the approval / control trail around the bypass is incomplete enough to require further investigation.

### Final document copy

**DELTA INDUSTRIAL NIGERIA LTD.**  
**PRELIMINARY INCIDENT REPORT — CONFIDENTIAL**

**Incident reference:** DIN/HSSE/IR-060126  
**Facility:** DIN Ogun State Production Facility  
**Date of incident:** 1 June 2026  
**Incident type:** Fatal workplace incident  
**Person affected:** Contract worker  
**Status:** Preliminary — investigation open

### 1. Incident summary

At approximately 14:20 on 1 June 2026, a contract worker was fatally injured during an operating incident on a production line at the DIN Ogun State facility. Emergency response procedures were activated, the affected line was isolated, and the relevant internal functions were notified.

### 2. Equipment condition identified during initial review

The initial technical inspection confirmed that a safety interlock intended to stop the line automatically under specified unsafe conditions was **not functioning in its normal protective mode at the time of the incident**.

The interlock had been placed under a temporary operating bypass. Records reviewed to date indicate that the bypass had remained in use beyond the period normally expected for a temporary deviation.

### 3. Approval and control trail

The current incident file contains references to operational approval for continued production while the interlock remained bypassed. The complete approval chain has **not yet been verified**.

The review team has requested all related maintenance records, operating-deviation approvals, supervisor logs, and management correspondence.

### 4. Immediate actions taken

- Production on the affected line suspended pending technical review.
- Relevant equipment isolated and secured.
- Contractor and family liaison process initiated.
- Internal HSSE investigation opened.
- Records relating to the interlock bypass placed under document hold.

### 5. Open questions

- When was the bypass first authorised?
- Who knew the interlock remained bypassed?
- Why was production allowed to continue?
- What earlier warnings or near-miss reports referred to the same protection system?
- Did commercial or production pressure influence the decision to keep the line running?

**Prepared for:** Board pre-review  
**Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL — NOT FOR EXTERNAL DISTRIBUTION

### Learner-highlight lines
When the document expands, lightly emphasise:

> “The safety interlock … was not functioning in its normal protective mode at the time of the incident.”

> “The complete approval chain has not yet been verified.”

### Interaction note
This document should **not** claim a final root cause. It should give enough verified information to justify further review without pre-judging the investigation.

## Evidence 02 — Near-Miss Report

### Purpose in the simulation
Shows that the same protective system had already produced a warning four months earlier, making the fatal incident harder to frame as unforeseeable or isolated.

### Final document copy

**DELTA INDUSTRIAL NIGERIA LTD.**  
**NEAR-MISS REPORT — INTERNAL USE ONLY**

**Reference:** DIN/HSSE/NMR-020426  
**Facility:** DIN Ogun State Production Facility  
**Date:** 4 February 2026  
**Classification:** High-potential near miss

### 1. Event summary

During routine production, an abnormal operating condition developed on the same production line later involved in the 1 June fatal incident. No employee or contractor was injured.

The line did not stop automatically at the point the operator expected the safety interlock to activate.

The operator initiated a manual stop and reported the event to the shift supervisor.

### 2. Initial technical observation

Maintenance confirmed that the interlock was operating under a temporary bypass pending planned maintenance work.

The line was returned to service after local checks were completed.

### 3. Recommended corrective actions

1. Restore the interlock to normal protective operation before continued routine production.
2. Confirm the approval basis for the temporary bypass.
3. Review whether production targets are affecting maintenance deferrals or operating-deviation decisions.
4. Escalate any extension of the bypass beyond the planned maintenance window.

### 4. Closure note recorded in the local file

> “Continue monitoring pending scheduled maintenance. Production commitments remain critical. Escalate if operating conditions deteriorate.”

### 5. Status at closure

**Administrative status:** Closed locally  
**Technical restoration confirmed:** No supporting record located in this file

### Learner-highlight lines

> “Technical restoration confirmed: No supporting record located in this file.”

### Interaction note
The learner should be able to visually connect this document to the Incident Report as part of the required **safety chain** review.

## Evidence 03 — Payment Records

### Purpose in the simulation
Confirms a repeated pattern of facilitation / expediting payments through a logistics agent over fourteen months, rather than a single isolated transaction.

### Production-authored fictional agent
**Crestfield Logistics Services Ltd.**

This name is fictional and used only to make the evidence document concrete.

### Final document copy

**DELTA INDUSTRIAL NIGERIA LTD.**  
**INTERNAL AUDIT EXTRACT — EXPEDITING FEE PAYMENTS**

**Reference:** DIN/IA/EF-062226  
**Review period:** April 2025 – May 2026  
**Prepared by:** Internal Audit  
**Status:** Escalation required

| Date | Payee | Budget line | Business description | Amount | Supporting documentation |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 10 Apr 2025 | Crestfield Logistics Services Ltd. | Expediting Fee | Customs clearance support | ₦4,800,000 | Agent invoice only |
| 17 Nov 2025 | Crestfield Logistics Services Ltd. | Expediting Fee | Local permit facilitation | ₦5,250,000 | Agent invoice + approval email |
| 28 May 2026 | Crestfield Logistics Services Ltd. | Expediting Fee | Priority clearance / local approvals | ₦6,100,000 | Agent invoice only |

### Audit observations

1. The same intermediary received three payments over approximately fourteen months.
2. The transactions were consistently charged to the **“Expediting Fee”** budget line.
3. The supporting files do not identify the ultimate recipient of funds beyond the logistics agent.
4. Business explanations refer to speeding up customs clearance and local permit processing.
5. Local management described the payments during audit interviews as part of **“how things get done here.”**
6. UK headquarters states it was not formally advised that the “Expediting Fee” budget line was being used for facilitation payments.

### Audit conclusion

The pattern is not sufficient, by itself, to establish an unlawful payment. It is sufficient to require Board-level review of:

- the purpose and destination of the payments;
- whether documentation standards were intentionally reduced;
- whether local practice had become normalised outside formal governance controls;
- whether reporting to group headquarters was adequate.

**Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL — INTERNAL AUDIT

### Learner-highlight lines

> “The same intermediary received three payments over approximately fourteen months.”

> “UK headquarters states it was not formally advised…”

### Interaction note
The UI should visually reveal the repeated pattern. The document must not state “bribery confirmed.” The learning problem is governance, complicity, and weak oversight under uncertainty.

# 5. Board Q&A — finalized answer system

The source defines **two Board questions**, with the opening question changing if the learner previously chose the direct-confrontation response to the Commercial Director.

To keep the implementation at **eight choice IDs total**, Question 1 uses the same option IDs `Q1-A` to `Q1-D`, but the displayed wording changes according to the branch.

Question 2 always uses `Q2-A` to `Q2-D`.

Feedback text is written as consequence and reasoning, never as a grade (script rule).

The Board Q&A should make the learner defend:

> **claim → evidence → trade-off → recommendation**

The best answer is not simply the most “ethical-sounding” answer. It should acknowledge the difficult trade-off and connect the learner's reasoning to evidence already generated in the simulation.

# 9. Ending resolver — required implementation clarification

The source provides three endings and their trigger thresholds, but the written threshold rules are **not mathematically exhaustive**. Some possible final combinations can fail to satisfy any of the three explicit conditions, even though the source also states that every path must resolve to an ending.

This is therefore a genuine implementation gap.

## Source-defined ending thresholds

### END-A — Defensible but costly
Trigger:

```ts
RT >= 65 && BC >= 55 && EM >= 55 && MN >= 55
```

### END-B — Reputation preserved, ethically thin
Trigger:

```ts
MN >= 60 && (RT < 50 || EM < 45)
```

### END-C — Collapse of credibility
Trigger:

```ts
RT < 40 || BC < 40
```

## Corrected deterministic resolver

Use the source thresholds exactly where they match. For states that match none of them, route by what the run actually earned:

- runs that meet END-A's **regulator and Board** thresholds (RT ≥ 65 and BC ≥ 55) but miss morale or media go to **END-A with a variant opening**;
- all other unmatched runs go to **END-B with a variant opening**.

```ts
function resolveEnding({ RT, BC, EM, MN }) {
  // Severe legitimacy failure has highest priority.
  if (RT < 40 || BC < 40) return { ending: 'END-C', variant: false };

  // Source-defined strong, defensible outcome.
  if (RT >= 65 && BC >= 55 && EM >= 55 && MN >= 55) return { ending: 'END-A', variant: false };

  // Source-defined thin-reputation outcome.
  if (MN >= 60 && (RT < 50 || EM < 45)) return { ending: 'END-B', variant: false };

  // Fallbacks required by the source rule that every run resolves.
  if (RT >= 65 && BC >= 55) return { ending: 'END-A', variant: true };
  return { ending: 'END-B', variant: true };
}
```

Variables are clamped to 0–100 after every change.

### Why this fallback
The original single fallback (always END-B) decided more than half of all possible runs and told about one run in nine — runs strong with the regulator and the Board — that its response was "ethically thin", which describes the opposite pattern. See section 19.12.

### Variant openings
- **END-A variant:** see section 10.
- **END-B variant:**
  > “The organisation avoids immediate collapse, but the Board's response leaves legitimacy uneven. Some audiences are reassured; others remain unconvinced.”

  Then continue with the source lesson:
  > “The public narrative may hold, but governance credibility remains thinner than it appears. This is the risk of treating reputation as proof of responsibility.”

This does **not** create a fourth ending.

# 10. Final ending copy

## END-A — Defensible but costly

### Title
**Defensible, but Costly**

### Outcome copy
The Board adopts your recommendation. DIN moves early with the regulator, funds the governance reforms, and publicly commits to a fuller account of what happened.

The result is not painless. Commercial relationships are strained, implementation costs rise, and some senior leaders challenge the direction. If the direct-confrontation path was taken, the Commercial Director may resign or be removed from the reform process.

Regulatory trust is preserved because the company chose to confront uncertainty rather than hide behind it.

### Closing lesson
> Ethical leadership does not eliminate trade-offs. It makes those trade-offs visible, defensible, and accountable.

### Variant opening (END-A reached through the fallback rule)
> “The Board adopts your recommendation, and it holds where it matters most — with the regulator and in the boardroom. It has not yet won over everyone it affects: {audience} still need to see the change before they believe it.”

`{audience}` = “employees” if EM < 55, “the public” if MN < 55, “employees and the public” if both.

### Narration
> “The Board chose the harder path — and it can defend it. What that cost is real. So is what it protected.”

---

## END-B — Reputation Preserved, Ethically Thin

### Title
**Reputation Preserved, Ethically Thin**

### Outcome copy
DIN avoids an immediate reputational collapse. Public messaging contains the story and the Board can point to formal action.

But the organisation has not fully repaired the deeper legitimacy problem. Employees or regulators remain unconvinced that the company has confronted the conditions that produced the crisis.

The response looks credible from a distance but is thinner when examined closely.

### Closing lesson
> Reputation can be managed faster than trust can be rebuilt. Governance becomes performative when the appearance of responsibility outruns the substance of change.

### Narration
> “The story held. The trust beneath it did not fully follow.”

---

## END-C — Collapse of Credibility

### Title
**Credibility Collapses**

### Outcome copy
Contradictions, delay, or unresolved accountability finally catch up with DIN.

The regulator opens a formal adversarial inquiry. Earlier statements are compared against internal records, and the Board's own decisions become part of the story.

The company can recover, but not cheaply. It now has to rebuild trust under external scrutiny rather than through voluntary reform.

### Closing lesson
> Governance failure compounds. The damage does not come from one decision alone, but from a pattern of choices that becomes impossible to defend together.

### Narration
> “The contradictions caught up. Now the Board must rebuild its credibility under someone else's scrutiny.”

## 19.2 Evidence review order flag

**Gap:** the script makes only the next correct step active and blocks wrong steps, which means every learner would finish "in the correct order" and `review_order_correct` could never be false.

**Decision:**
- Later steps are visible but greyed. Activating one shows the script's blocking message and increments `evidenceBlockedAttempts`.
- `review_order_correct = true` only when all four steps are completed **and** `evidenceBlockedAttempts = 0`.

**Steps:**
1. Verify the safety chain of command — Incident Report + Near-Miss Report (both)
2. Trace the financial record — Payment Records
3. Review internal correspondence — Internal Correspondence
4. Check external exposure — News Clipping

**Blocking message (source):**
> "You don't have enough internal verification yet to interpret what the press does or doesn't know. Complete your internal review first."

**Completion — correct order (source):**
> "You've completed the review in a defensible sequence — internal facts first, external framing last. You have a credible evidence base."

**Completion — after a blocked attempt (production-authored):**
> "Your review is complete, and your evidence base is sound. But you reached for a later step before the internal record was verified — notice that pull. It is how narratives get ahead of findings."

**Feedback (source):**
> "Verifying internal facts before checking external exposure is standard professional practice. Reviewing external press first leads to 'narrative-first' thinking — exactly the trap that produced the 'keep documentation light' instruction. A defensible review is the foundation of credible Board advice."

## 19.3 Accountability model — sliders, GRI, RCS, Board Credibility award

**Gap:** the script requires a live Governance Risk Index (GRI) and Reform Credibility Score (RCS) and awards "BC +5 to +15 depending on how well-supported" the Round 2 allocation is, but defines no formulas.

### Levers
| Lever | Label | Plain-language subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| `agency` | Agency failure | Local management pursued cost and speed at the organisation's expense; HQ monitoring didn't catch it. |
| `stewardship` | Stewardship failure | Local management believed it was acting for the company but had no safe channel to raise concerns. |
| `stakeholderRecognition` | Stakeholder-recognition failure | No one in the chain treated the worker or the regulator as a legitimate claim-holder. |

### Slider behaviour
- Whole percentages, 0–100, always totalling 100.
- Moving one lever redistributes the difference across the other two in proportion to their current values (evenly if both are 0).
- Starting allocation for Round 1: 34 / 33 / 33. Round 2 starts from the locked Round 1 allocation.
- Keyboard: arrow keys ±1, Page Up / Page Down ±10.

### Reference evidence profiles (never shown to the learner)
| Round | Agency | Stewardship | Stakeholder-recognition |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Round 1 (five evidence documents) | 35 | 30 | 35 |
| Round 2 (after the supervisor statement) | 45 | 20 | 35 |

These encode the script's statement that "the Round 2 evidence shifts the weight of the written record toward Agency failure, but a balanced diagnosis still accounts for the systemic pressures that enabled it."

### Formulas
```ts
// d = distance from the round's evidence profile, 0–100
d = (|agency − A*| + |stewardship − S*| + |stakeholderRecognition − K*|) / 2
singleCause = any lever is 0 or 100
unchanged   = round === 2 && allocation equals Round 1 exactly

GRI = clamp(round(25 + 0.9·d + (singleCause ? 20 : 0)), 0, 100)   // lower is better
RCS = clamp(round(100 − 1.1·d − (singleCause ? 15 : 0) − (unchanged ? 10 : 0)), 0, 100) // higher is better
```

Displayed bands (words beside the live number):
- GRI: 0–39 **Contained** · 40–64 **Elevated** · 65–100 **Severe**
- RCS: 70–100 **Credible** · 45–69 **Contestable** · 0–44 **Weak**

GRI and RCS are model outputs the script requires to be visible. They are not the hidden state variables.

### Board Credibility award (applied once, on Round 2 submit)
```ts
respondsToEvidence = round2.agency − round1.agency >= 5
if (!respondsToEvidence)            BC += 5    // script: ignoring Round 2 evidence caps the gain at +5
else if (d2 <= 15 && !singleCause2) BC += 15
else if (d2 <= 30 && !singleCause2) BC += 10
else                                BC += 5
```

### Messages
- Single-cause warning (source, shown whenever a lever is at 0% or 100%; does not block):
  > "A single-cause diagnosis is rarely credible to a Board that has seen the full evidence file — consider whether this allocation survives challenge."
- Round 2, Agency up by 5 or more (source):
  > "Your Agency-failure weighting moved from X% to Y% — this is now better supported by written evidence, not inference."
- Round 2, allocation unchanged (source):
  > "An unchanged diagnosis in the face of new evidence may look like motivated reasoning to the Board."
- Round 2, changed but Agency not up by 5 (production-authored):
  > "Your Agency-failure weighting moved from X% to Y%. The supervisor's statement puts a written approval above plant level on the record — be ready to explain why your weighting does not reflect it."
- Feedback (source):
  > "Governance failures are rarely monocausal. A defensible diagnosis weighs evidence and adjusts when new information arrives — exactly the process a Board expects from its advisor. The Round 2 evidence shifts the weight of the written record toward Agency failure, but a balanced diagnosis still accounts for the systemic pressures that enabled it."

### Primary failure
The highest-weighted lever in Round 2. Ties count every tied lever as primary.
